# The Space Race and the Apollo Program

## Origins of the Space Race

The Space Race was a Cold War-era competition between the United States and
the Soviet Union to achieve superior spaceflight capability. It began in
1957, when the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, the first artificial satellite
to orbit Earth, catching the United States off guard.

## Early Soviet Firsts

The Soviet Union extended its early lead in 1961, when cosmonaut Yuri
Gagarin became the first human to travel into space and orbit the Earth,
aboard Vostok 1. This success pushed the United States to accelerate its own
crewed spaceflight program.

## The Apollo Program

In a 1961 speech to Congress, President John F. Kennedy committed the United
States to landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely to Earth before
the end of the decade. NASA's Apollo program was built around that goal,
following the earlier Mercury and Gemini programs.

## Apollo 11 Moon Landing

On July 20, 1969, the Apollo 11 mission's lunar module Eagle landed in the
Sea of Tranquility. Neil Armstrong became the first human to step onto the
lunar surface, followed shortly after by Buzz Aldrin, while Michael Collins
orbited above in the command module, call-signed Columbia.

## Legacy of the Apollo Program

The Apollo program continued through six successful crewed landings, ending
with Apollo 17 in December 1972. In total, twelve astronauts walked on the
Moon's surface, and no human has returned since.
