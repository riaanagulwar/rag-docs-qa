# The Roman Empire

## Founding and the Republic

According to tradition, Rome was founded in 753 BC. The Roman Republic began
in 509 BC, when the last king, Tarquin the Proud, was overthrown and replaced
with a system of elected magistrates called consuls, advised by the Senate.
This republican system lasted for almost 500 years.

## Julius Caesar and the End of the Republic

Julius Caesar crossed the Rubicon river with his army in 49 BC, starting a
civil war that ended the Republic in all but name. He was declared dictator
for life in 44 BC, but was assassinated that same year on the Ides of March
(March 15th) by a group of senators, including Brutus and Cassius, who
feared he intended to make himself king.

## Augustus and the Pax Romana

Caesar's adopted heir, Octavian, defeated his rivals and in 27 BC became
Rome's first emperor under the name Augustus. His reign began roughly two
centuries of relative internal peace and stability across the empire known
as the Pax Romana.

## The Fall of the Western Roman Empire

The Western Roman Empire is traditionally said to have fallen in 476 AD,
when the Germanic chieftain Odoacer deposed the last Western emperor, a
teenager named Romulus Augustulus, and declared himself King of Italy.

## Roman Engineering and Roads

The Romans built an extensive network of roads, eventually covering roughly
250,000 miles, along with aqueducts that carried water into cities over long
distances. Their use of concrete, known as opus caementicium, allowed
structures like the Pantheon and the Colosseum to survive for two thousand
years.
